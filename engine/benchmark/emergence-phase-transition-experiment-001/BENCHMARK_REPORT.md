# Emergence Phase Transition Experiment 001 — Benchmark Report

## Executive summary

**Classification: D — Production Bottleneck Dominates.**

No configuration across four independent mechanism families passed all nine
operational emergence criteria. Consequently, no first-passage point and no
reproducible phase boundary were observed.

Relational topology still mattered strongly. Connected fixtures exceeded their
matched disconnected controls by **5.5 criteria on average**, and retaining a
critical bridge exceeded bridge removal by **5.5 criteria on average**.
However, those changes stopped short of complete predictive and intervention
utility. The experiment therefore detected a topology-sensitive production
response, not end-to-end emergence.

## Production path audit

Raw Evidence entered unchanged production `runDiscoveryV3()` and then
`evolveOrganizationRuntime()` against a new in-memory Runtime. Production
generated every cognition artifact used for capability scoring. No producers,
schemas, Runtime contracts, capabilities, or existing benchmarks changed.

The analysis graph used only raw lexical overlap, source silo membership,
timestamps, and production-generated lineage. It did not receive scoring truth.
The replay is a true isolated production replay, not a simulated cognition
path.

These are sharp partial-capability changes. They are not phase transitions
under the predefined operational definition because prediction and/or
intervention remained incomplete.

## Constant-quantity topology test

Connected and disconnected pairs matched Evidence count, silo count, source
reliability, timestamps, and approximate character volume. The intended
difference was relational topology.

Commercial, escalation, and customer families gained seven criteria in the
connected configuration. Optimization gained one. This falsifies an
Evidence-count-only explanation, but does not prove causal sufficiency:
connectivity enabled production structure without completing organizational
utility.

## Critical bridge and peripheral removal

Bridge removal and equally sized peripheral removal were compared in every
family. The average differential was +5.5 criteria in favor of preserving the
bridge. Commercial, escalation, and customer cases were strongly bridge
sensitive; optimization remained limited because production composed only the
downstream portion of its longer chain.

The most consequential bridge type was the middle relationship translating an
upstream functional choice into a downstream operating effect:

- customization → implementation variability;
- visible risk plus missing information → escalation;
- lead-time variability → schedule variability;
- adoption shortfall → training demand.

No single bridge generalized as a universal semantic relationship; the shared
property was causal-cluster connection.

## Redundancy, complementarity, and noise

Equal-count complementary configurations consistently produced more
production structure than repeated local Evidence. Repetition could increase
mechanism confidence without supplying the missing relationship. This confirms
that confidence accumulation is not emergence.

Noise did not form a clean universal inverted-U:

- commercial cognition remained structurally active while semantic
  completeness fluctuated;
- customer and optimization sometimes formed additional mechanisms only after
  more noise, demonstrating false relational opportunity rather than useful
  emergence;
- escalation lost discriminability as alternatives accumulated.

The benchmark therefore finds family-specific degradation and occasional
noise-enabled synthesis, not evidence for a general “edge of chaos.”

## Alternative-mechanism competition

Adding equally plausible staffing explanations reduced discriminability or
increased mechanism count, but production did not consistently lower
confidence or abstain. Relational richness and resolvable evidence should not
be conflated: more candidate relationships sometimes created more generated
mechanisms without improving the correct explanation.

## Temporal structure

Correct, ambiguous, reverse, and absent timestamps produced nearly identical
production cognition within each family. The analysis graph recorded temporal
differences, but production output generally did not use them to adjudicate
causal order. Temporal support was therefore not a controlling transition
variable in this replay—even for causal chains where it should matter.

## Producer trace

The exact traces are retained in `RESULTS.json`. Across the strongest partial
cases:

1. Evidence preserved the intended local statements.
2. Observations and Signals retained local semantic fragments.
3. Themes or Phenomena represented broad organizational patterns.
4. Mechanism formation was the first stage where a cross-silo relationship
   appeared.
5. Mechanisms frequently selected an adjacent segment rather than composing
   the complete upstream-to-outcome chain.
6. Downstream prediction and recommendation often generalized toward canonical
   organizational conditions rather than preserving the scenario-specific
   mechanism.

The false-positive opportunity first appeared at Mechanism formation when
dense or noisy support was treated as sufficient cross-silo structure without
complete discriminability.

## Metric analysis

No metric predicted positive first-passage emergence because there were no
positive first passages. Apparent threshold accuracies near 1.0 came from
predicting the dominant “no emergence” class and are explicitly rejected as
scientific evidence.

Metrics that tracked partial capability:

- qualifying cross-silo mechanism count;
- maximum supporting silo count;
- lexical complementarity;
- critical-bridge preservation;
- production lineage completeness.

Misleading metrics:

- Evidence count;
- silo count;
- character volume;
- graph density alone;
- mechanism confidence;
- raw mechanism count;
- contradiction count.

Leave-one-family-out threshold results are not meaningful evidence of
generalization under an all-negative operational outcome.

## Negative controls

No negative-control configuration passed all emergence criteria. Dense graphs,
repeated Evidence, reversed temporal order, ambiguous alternatives, and noise
therefore did not create a full false-positive success. Some created partial
mechanisms and increased confidence, confirming why connectivity-only and
confidence-only metrics would be unsafe.

## Verbal boundary comparison

### Commercial complexity

Before the partial boundary, Discovery could support local exception and
customization relationships but not the full downstream consequence. After the
bridge, production linked Sales, Product, Operations, and Finance Evidence and
predicted higher implementation cost and lower margin. It still did not select
the scenario-specific exception-standardization intervention.

### Decision escalation

Before the partial boundary, Discovery saw risk-sensitive promotion behavior
and concentrated information separately. After the bridge, it connected these
conditions to manager escalation. The integrated explanation was new and
cross-silo, but the downstream intervention remained generic.

### Local optimization

Before the partial boundary, production retained silo-local cost and lead-time
observations. Even after all Evidence arrived, it formed only the downstream
schedule → missed commitment → expedite-cost segment. The missing request just
below the boundary is evidence directly connecting supplier lead-time
variability to operating schedule variability.

### Customer misdiagnosis

Before the partial boundary, workflow confusion and low adoption remained
local. After the bridge, production connected Product, Sales, and Customer
Success into training pressure, but did not reliably preserve the final
retention implication or select workflow/usability intervention.

All exact generated wording and lineage are retained in the machine result.
The verbal projection adds no mechanism semantics.

## Determinism and leakage

All leakage checks passed. All order and stability checks passed:

- repeated byte equality;
- reversed configuration order;
- reversed Evidence order;
- reversed silo and within-silo order;
- stable graph construction;
- stable metrics;
- stable producer traces;
- stable verbal output.

All twelve hard gates passed.

## Required final answers

1. **Reproducible phase transition?** No.
2. **Without increasing quantity?** Topology caused large partial changes at
   constant quantity, but no full transition.
3. **Most consequential bridge?** The relationship connecting an upstream
   functional choice to its downstream operating effect.
4. **Best first-passage metric?** None; there was no first passage.
5. **Misleading metrics?** Quantity, silo count, density, confidence, mechanism
   count, and contradiction count.
6. **Complementarity versus redundancy?** Complementarity was stronger.
7. **Connectivity false positives?** It created partial mechanisms and
   confidence, but no full operational false positive.
8. **Completeness versus density?** Causal completeness was more important.
9. **Temporal structure required?** Production did not respond reliably to it.
10. **Contradictions?** Contradiction count alone did not discriminate;
    resolvability was not preserved strongly enough to test a threshold.
11. **Inverted-U?** No universal inverted-U; degradation was family-specific.
12. **Controlling stage?** Mechanism formation enabled partial transitions;
    downstream prediction and intervention prevented completion.
13. **Predictable before formation?** Bridge preservation and complementarity
    indicated readiness for partial structure, not full emergence.
14. **Generalized rule?** No operational rule generalized.
15. **Conclusion?** No reliable full threshold; production currently obscures
    any underlying boundary.
16. **Missing relationship request?** Ask for direct Evidence connecting the
    last supported local effect to the next downstream outcome.
17. **What does it sound like?** “Each team’s observation is locally valid, but
    Discovery cannot yet support the complete explanation. The missing test is
    whether the operating effect actually produces the downstream outcome.”
18. **Next test?** A benchmark-only Mechanism composition trace that supplies
    no new semantics but measures why adjacent generated Mechanisms are not
    composed into complete causal chains.

## Decision

Do not claim a universal or scenario-specific phase boundary. Do not implement
an Emergence Score, graph architecture, or cognition layer. The minimum next
step is a benchmark-only Mechanism composition/adjudication experiment focused
on complete causal-chain preservation and scenario-specific downstream
handoff.
