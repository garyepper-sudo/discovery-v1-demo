# Localized Nonlinear Cognition Experiment 001 — Benchmark Report

## Executive Summary

Classification:

```text
A — Localized Nonlinear Cognition Demonstrated
```

Localized nonlinear cognition materially outperformed both a strong stable
linear baseline and pervasive nonlinear cognition while preserving deterministic
behavior, lineage, reversibility, abstention, and interpretability.

| Architecture | Utility | Prediction MAE | Transition precision | Transition recall | Negative-control precision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stable linear | 0.533 | 0.154 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 1.000 |
| Localized nonlinear | 0.975 | 0.008 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Pervasive nonlinear | 0.610 | 0.200 | 0.667 | 0.889 | 0.250 |

The result supports a benchmark-local architectural hypothesis only. It does not
authorize production adoption.

## Localized versus stable linear

Localized cognition added:

- qualified Mechanism emergence after complementary relational sufficiency;
- state-sensitive activation, inhibition, and amplification;
- state transition and evidence-backed reversal;
- mechanism confirmation, weakening, retirement, and alternative promotion;
- improved later predictions across deterministic outcome cycles.

The stable baseline remained safe and interpretable but missed all 36 required
qualitative transitions and had materially higher prediction error.

## Localized versus pervasive nonlinear

Localized cognition produced no false transitions. Pervasive nonlinearity
produced:

- 16 false transitions;
- 6 false nonlinear control classifications;
- negative-control precision of `0.25`;
- prediction MAE of `0.200`;
- a confidence-cascade rate of `15.8%`.

Broad lower-layer promotion allowed redundancy, noise, external shock, correlated
Conditions, and unregistered feedback to acquire causal authority.

## Outcome-driven learning

Preregistered discriminating outcomes:

- confirmed a leading Mechanism;
- weakened it and promoted an alternative;
- retired both explanations when neither prediction succeeded;
- left structure unchanged when the outcome was ambiguous.

Multi-cycle confidence remained bounded, retained original lineage, avoided
runaway confidence and oscillation, and allowed a later outcome to restore the
leading explanation.

## Reversibility and sequencing

Contradictory support reversed an earlier Organizational State transition.
Every transition recorded a reason, lineage, and explicit reversal criterion.
State interaction was blocked before Mechanism qualification. Structural
revision was blocked before prediction registration.

## Ablation results

| Ablation | Utility | Transition recall | Prediction MAE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| No Mechanism formation | 0.766 | 0.194 | 0.090 |
| No State interaction | 0.904 | 0.833 | 0.058 |
| No Outcome revision | 0.908 | 0.806 | 0.043 |
| Formation only | 0.837 | 0.639 | 0.093 |
| Formation + State | 0.908 | 0.806 | 0.043 |
| Formation + Outcome | 0.904 | 0.833 | 0.058 |

Removing Mechanism-formation nonlinearity caused the largest loss. Formation is
the foundational zone. State interaction contributes regime-sensitive
prediction; outcome revision contributes adaptation. All three are required for
the full operational definition of dynamical cognition.

## Interpretability and stability

Localized transition lineage, explicit-reason, and reversal-criterion rates were
all `1.00`. Repeated execution and reversed scenario, Evidence, and silo ordering
were byte-identical. Ablations, multi-cycle revisions, and verbal explanations
were stable.

## Final answers

1. **Nonlinearity everywhere?** No.
2. **Beneficial levels:** Mechanism formation, qualified Mechanism–State
   interaction, and post-prediction outcome revision.
3. **Harmed levels:** Lower Evidence/Observation promotion and broad recursive
   confidence feedback.
4. **Formation alone sufficient?** It produces emergence, but not full
   state-sensitive and adaptive cognition.
5. **State interaction improves prediction?** Yes.
6. **Outcome revision improves later reasoning?** Yes.
7. **All three zones required?** Yes for the complete dynamical definition.
8. **Largest ablation loss:** Mechanism formation.
9. **Most false positives:** Pervasive lower-layer promotion and broad feedback.
10. **Pervasive instability/path dependence:** It caused false transitions and
    confidence cascades; ordering remained deterministic in this implementation.
11. **Localized determinism and interpretability:** Preserved.
12. **State transitions reversible:** Yes.
13. **Mechanisms retired after falsification:** Yes.
14. **Alternatives promoted safely:** Yes, only after registered discriminating outcomes.
15. **Multi-cycle improvement:** Demonstrated.
16. **Cascades and oscillation avoided:** Yes.
17. **Few transition points sufficient:** Yes within the controlled corpus.
18. **Minimal safe architecture:** Stable representation plus nonlinear
    Mechanism formation; add state interaction for regime-sensitive prediction
    and outcome revision for adaptation.
19. **Unsupported contracts:** Lower-layer nonlinearity, unregistered recursive
    feedback, response-shape-only dynamics, and unrestricted natural-language formation.
20. **Next test:** Independent, less structured scenario replication of the
    three localized contracts with unchanged thresholds.
21. **Production proposal justified:** No.

## Scientific conclusion

The experiment provides benchmark evidence that a mostly stable cognition chain
can exhibit globally dynamic, adaptive behavior when nonlinearity is localized
to a small number of explicit, reversible, lineage-preserving transition
contracts. Synthetic success is not sufficient for production authorization.
