# Organizational Objective Discovery Experiment 001 — Report

## Result

Architecture G, governed hybrid adaptive discovery, wins the bounded synthetic benchmark. It preserves multiple plausible objective hypotheses, treats declarations, evidence, decisions, strategies, metrics, and conditions as signals, separates authority from evidential confidence, and asks only the highest-value unresolved question.

This is benchmark evidence for a future product contract, not production performance and not permission to infer an organizational objective automatically.

## Boundary

- Objective hypotheses are temporary, non-authoritative discovery projections.
- A declaration is a signal whose authority must be verified.
- Observed behavior is evidence of a possible objective, never authority for one.
- Only a confirmed, scoped, authorized objective becomes governed.
- Conflicting objectives remain explicit until an authorized resolution or scoped coexistence is recorded.
- Volatility triggers revalidation; it does not silently replace the governed objective.

## Comparison

The generated metrics are emitted by `run.ts`. Architectures A–E each lose material information because they accept one source family. Architecture F preserves ambiguity but cannot distinguish strong evidence from governing authority. Architecture G adds exactly that missing governance boundary.

The full questionnaire is accurate but burdensome. The fixed interview is concise but misses scenario-dependent ambiguity. The hypothesis interview improves recovery but still lacks authority correctness. The hybrid adaptive interaction matches full-form correctness while asking only the material question in these fixtures.

## Limits

Fixtures are authored and deterministic, not a customer study. Recommendation quality is measured only as downstream eligibility readiness; no recommendation is generated. Human-expert comparison is conceptual and must be validated in field research.
