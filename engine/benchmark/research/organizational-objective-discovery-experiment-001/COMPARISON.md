# Objective Discovery Architecture Comparison

| Architecture | Strength | Failure mode | Research disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Direct declaration | Simple and explicit | Confuses statement with authority; misses conflict and change | Insufficient alone |
| B — Evidence inference | Grounded in observed information | Evidence cannot authorize an end | Signal only |
| C — Decision inference | Reveals enacted priorities | Past choices may be constrained or obsolete | Signal only |
| D — Strategy inference | Often close to intended direction | Strategy may be stale, aspirational, or a means | Signal only |
| E — Metric inference | Measurable and easy to monitor | KPI is not necessarily the objective; Goodhart risk | Signal only |
| F — Multiple hypotheses | Preserves ambiguity and supports discriminating questions | Does not separate confidence from authority | Reusable research pattern |
| G — Governed hybrid adaptive | Preserves hypotheses, authority, change, and efficiency | Requires a bounded discovery contract | Benchmark winner |

## Interaction comparison

The canonical candidate interaction is adaptive: reuse existing authorized signals, present the smallest set of materially distinct hypotheses, ask one question that resolves the greatest authority or meaning ambiguity, and stop when the objective is governable or truthful abstention is required. It must never turn evidence volume into authority.
