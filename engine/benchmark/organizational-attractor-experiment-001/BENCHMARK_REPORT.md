# Organizational Attractor Experiment 001 — Benchmark Report

## Result

**Controlled experimental score:** 100 / 100

**Hard gates:** 6 / 6 pass

**Negative-control false positives:** 0 / 4

**Architecture promotion:** Not authorized

The Candidate Attractor representation adds prediction value inside the
controlled experimental model. The result does not yet establish incremental
value over the actual production cognition chain.

## Observed evidence

No source states that the organization wants or possesses centralized control.
The baseline is distributed across:

| Evidence | Independent source | Observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | board minutes | Three delegation programs ended when executives resumed exception approval |
| E2 | manager interviews | Managers send uncertain decisions upward |
| E3 | performance policy | Visible mistakes are penalized more than delayed escalation |
| E4 | information audit | Trade-off information remains concentrated among executives |
| E5 | employee pulse | Teams wait for approval under ambiguous ownership |
| E6 | leadership town hall | Executives publicly request more local decision-making |

E6 is retained as opposing Evidence rather than discarded.

## Inferred candidate

```text
Target state:
centralized-decision-control

Persistence confidence:
0.95 baseline
0.855322 after short-term delegation

Supporting Evidence:
E1, E2, E3, E4, E5

Opposing Evidence:
E6 baseline
E6, E7, E8, E9 after perturbation
```

Restoring mechanisms:

1. executive override;
2. error penalty;
3. information concentration;
4. managerial risk avoidance;
5. approval dependency.

Activating conditions include ambiguous decisions, visible failure risk,
cross-functional trade-offs, and unclear ownership.

Falsification requires observable structural change:

- distribute the information required for consequential trade-offs;
- consistently decline executive override of contested local decisions;
- reward learning from reversible local mistakes.

## Competing explanations

| Model | Explanation |

| Organizational State | The currently observed decentralized behavior persists |
| Mechanisms | The direction of mechanisms active in the latest period persists at every horizon |
| Candidate Attractor | Persistent structural mechanisms can restore a direction after temporary displacement |

The models deliberately make different temporal commitments.

## Critical evaluation

### Did the candidate add understanding?

Inside this controlled representation, yes. It adds an explicit distinction
between:

- current behavior;
- currently active mechanisms;
- a persistent, mechanism-grounded direction with a restoration timescale.

That distinction produces two additional correct trajectory predictions in
the synthetic branches.

### Did it prove a new production cognitive layer?

No.

The experimental fixture supplies normalized direction, structural status,
period, mechanism, and condition labels. Production Evidence does not
currently provide this exact input contract. The State and Mechanism comparison
models are controlled baselines, not replays of the full production
Organizational State and Mechanism producers.

The candidate may therefore be:

1. a useful new abstraction; or
2. temporal composition of existing Mechanisms, Conditions, State, and
   Prediction capabilities.

Experiment 001 cannot distinguish those explanations. A perfect controlled
score should not be mistaken for production readiness.

### Construct-validity risk

The benchmark's hidden truth and inference features were designed together.
Although no source names the attractor, the structured `direction`,
`structural`, and `mechanism` fields make the latent pattern easier to recover
than raw organizational Evidence would.

The result establishes algorithmic feasibility and useful failure criteria. It
does not establish ecological validity.

## Recommendation

Keep Organizational Attractors research-only.

The next experiment should be a held-out, production-input shadow:

1. derive candidate inputs only from unchanged production Evidence,
   Mechanisms, Conditions, State, and Prediction objects;
2. hide branch outcomes from the candidate adapter;
3. include multiple attractor families beyond decision centralization;
4. compare against full production-derived baselines;
5. test duplicate sources, missing periods, delayed structural change, and
   adversarial but plausible narratives;
6. require the candidate to outperform a temporal Mechanism-composition
   baseline;
7. preserve abstention as a hard gate.

Until that experiment passes, do not add an Organizational Attractor object or
layer to production cognition.
