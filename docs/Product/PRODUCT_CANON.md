# Discovery Product Canon

**Status:** Canonical

---

# Purpose

This document defines Discovery's permanent product philosophy.

It answers one question:

> **What is Discovery?**

Every product decision, engineering investment, workflow, executive experience, benchmark, and customer validation effort should reinforce the principles defined here.

Unlike sprint documentation, this document changes rarely.

If another product document conflicts with this one, this document is correct.

This authority is limited to product identity, product philosophy, user value,
product posture, and product-level application lifecycles. Specific canonical
architecture documents govern their named technical domains, including
security, Runtime, cognition, governance, tenancy, persistence, and the
cross-application platform learning lifecycle. Broad product prose does not
override those contracts.

---

# Mission

> **Discovery is a platform that continuously builds shared organizational intelligence that becomes more valuable than the sum of its contributors, information, and experiences.**

Discovery helps organizations continuously grow shared intelligence. It accomplishes this by continuously improving an organization's understanding of itself.

Discovery does not replace executive decision-making.

It improves executive decision-making by helping organizations better understand reality, make better commitments, learn from outcomes, and continuously improve over time.

Every completed organizational cycle should leave both the organization and Discovery more capable than before.

---

# Product Identity

Discovery is a **shared organizational intelligence platform**.

Its first major application is the **Executive Operating System**, which remains the current product focus and helps organizations continuously evolve through better executive understanding, decisions, simulation, communication, and learning.

Its purpose is to help organizations continuously evolve.

Discovery is not:

- a dashboard,
- a chatbot,
- a reporting platform,
- a document repository,
- a project management system,
- a business intelligence platform,
- an autonomous executive.

Discovery exists to help organizations:

- understand reality,
- manage Executive Work,
- improve executive judgment,
- execute intentionally,
- learn continuously,
- strengthen future organizational performance.

---

# The Organization Is The Product

Discovery is not organized around pages.

Discovery is organized around organizations.

Everything inside Discovery exists to improve one continuously evolving organization.

The organization—not the interface—is the center of the product.

---

# The Organization Model and Operating Model

Discovery's model terminology follows this hierarchy:

- **Shared Organizational Intelligence** is the broader platform-level
  intelligence construct.
- **Organization Runtime** is the canonical technical persistence boundary.
- **Organization Model** is the user-facing living representation of how the
  organization is understood.
- **Operating Model** is the operational representation within the broader
  Organization Model and Organizational Intelligence.

The Organization Model may include evidence, insights, contradictions,
conditions, decisions, initiatives, outcomes, learning, uncertainty, and
organizational memory. It is a product representation, not a new persistence
object or a second Runtime.

The Operating Model focuses on how the organization currently operates,
including:

- structures,
- behaviors,
- constraints,
- mechanisms,
- coordination,
- execution,
- learning patterns.

Decisions, initiatives, outcomes, simulations, uncertainty, provenance, and
broader organizational memory may interact with and inform the Operating Model
without being reducible to it. The Organization Model and Operating Model do
not require separate Runtime objects.

Everything Discovery produces must remain grounded in canonical Organizational
Intelligence.

Including:

- executive understanding,
- recommendations,
- simulations,
- communication,
- confidence,
- executive learning.

Every warranted improvement to the Operating Model should improve future
organizational understanding and, within the Executive Operating System,
support better executive decisions.

The Operating Model is Discovery's primary competitive advantage.

# Participation and Adoption

Participants contribute observations, evidence, questions, hypotheses, corrections, decisions, and outcomes. Discovery synthesizes organizational understanding through provenance, corroboration, confidence, contradiction handling, uncertainty, evidence quality, and explicit commitment boundaries.

The product may begin with one meaningful problem for an individual, team, department, initiative, functional leader, or executive group. It should earn trust, discover adjacent relationships, expand participation, and build richer shared intelligence without assuming organization-wide or executive-only adoption.

Participants do not directly modify organizational truth. Discovery represents its current best understanding, not absolute truth.

# Executive Work

Within the Executive Operating System application, Executive Work is the primary organizational object managed inside Discovery.

Executive Work represents intentional organizational change.

Executive Work includes:

- executive decisions,
- executive initiatives,
- Discovery recommendations,
- executive-created work.

Discovery exists to improve Executive Work throughout its lifecycle.

---

# Organizational Lifecycle

Organizations continuously evolve.

Discovery continuously evolves with them.

The canonical product lifecycle for the Executive Operating System application
is:

```text
Organization

↓

Operating Model

↓

Continuous Understanding

↓

Executive Work

↓

Decision Support

↓

Executive Commitment

↓

Execution

↓

Review

↓

Learning

↓

Operating Model Evolution

↓

Repeat
```

Discovery succeeds when organizations repeatedly complete this lifecycle and improve over time.

This is an application-specific use of the broader Universal Intelligence
Lifecycle. Not every platform learning cycle must become Executive Work, and
not every investigation must become a decision. Other applications may
instantiate the platform lifecycle through different interactions and actions
without weakening this lifecycle's authority inside the Executive Operating
System.

---

# Executive Judgment

Executive judgment is the north star of Discovery's first major application.

Discovery improves judgment by helping executives:

- understand more accurately,
- recognize organizational constraints,
- evaluate alternatives,
- understand consequences,
- commit confidently,
- continuously learn.

The objective is not better recommendations.

The objective is better organizations.

---

# Executive Experience

Discovery should always answer the next executive question.

Executives naturally think:

```text
What changed?

↓

Why?

↓

What deserves attention?

↓

What should we do?

↓

What happens if...?

↓

Are we ready?

↓

How is execution progressing?

↓

What did we learn?
```

The application should naturally guide executives through this sequence.

---

# Continuous Understanding

Organizations never stop changing.

Discovery should never stop learning.

New evidence continuously improves:

- organizational understanding,
- confidence,
- recommendations,
- Executive Work,
- future executive judgment.

Executives should never need to "rerun Discovery."

The Operating Model continuously evolves.

---

# Progressive Disclosure

Discovery minimizes executive effort.

Default experience:

```text
Summary

↓

Recommendation

↓

Explanation

↓

Supporting Reasoning
```

Complexity should remain optional.

Executives should never be overwhelmed.

---

# Executive Effort

Discovery should infer as much context as possible.

The default interaction model is:

```text
Discovery Understands

↓

Executive Confirms

↓

Discovery Reasons

↓

Executive Commits

↓

Discovery Learns
```

Executives should spend their time making organizational commitments rather than entering information.

---

# Trust

Trust is Discovery's most valuable product asset.

Discovery earns trust through:

- transparent reasoning,
- explicit uncertainty,
- confidence calibration,
- organizational learning,
- observable improvement over time.

Trust should increase because Discovery continuously proves itself against reality.

---

# Confidence

Confidence exists to improve judgment.

Confidence should always answer:

- Why?
- Compared to what?
- What assumptions matter?
- What evidence is missing?
- What would change this recommendation?

Confidence should never exist to persuade.

It exists to improve executive understanding.

---

# Organizational Memory

Discovery becomes the organization's long-term memory.

It remembers:

- evidence,
- executive work,
- decisions,
- initiatives,
- simulations,
- assumptions,
- outcomes,
- reviews,
- learning.

Organizations become progressively better because Discovery remembers what organizations naturally forget.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Discovery compounds in value through repetition.

```text
Executive Work

↓

Execution

↓

Review

↓

Learning

↓

Operating Model

↓

Better Executive Judgment

↓

Better Executive Work
```

Every completed cycle should strengthen the next.

This sequence describes continuous improvement within the Executive Operating
System. Other applications participate in the same platform learning lifecycle
without being required to create Executive Work.

---

# Product Principles

Every feature should strengthen at least one of:

- organizational understanding,
- Executive Work,
- executive judgment,
- organizational learning,
- Operating Model quality,
- executive trust,
- confidence,
- longitudinal organizational improvement.

If it strengthens none of these, it should not exist.

---

# Product Success

Discovery succeeds when:

- organizations naturally manage Executive Work inside Discovery,
- executives trust Discovery's understanding,
- organizations continuously improve their Operating Model,
- every completed Executive Work item improves future executive judgment,
- Discovery demonstrably becomes better as organizations evolve.

Discovery's success is measured not by the quality of individual recommendations, but by whether shared Organizational Intelligence becomes richer, more connected, more trustworthy, and more valuable over time—and improves organizational judgment as a result.
